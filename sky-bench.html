Rolls through the SHIPPED generator, so what you see is something a race could actually produce. The hour sets the region, a family sets how the horizon derives from the zenith. Stage one — the palette

The palette is what you ASK FOR. N entries in, N colours out — no quantisation, nothing to deduce.
At a dozen colours a palette is a LIST to be stated, not a ramp to be interpolated. Africa_Stage1's steps run 0.0013, 0.0024, 0.0037, 0.0056, then JUMP 0.0677 — a 54x ratio. Pinning the jump catches the discontinuity; the segments between pins are still walked evenly, and four of eleven entries came out wrong.

Stage two — the distribution

CROSSFADE spends a share of each band fading into the next, with a duty cycle that RAMPS — A holds while B flickers, then the other way round. That is how the references get sixteen apparent steps out of four colours; the older `dither` is a fixed flicker at the boundary only. CHECKER renders the middle (checkerboard retired 2026-08-25; fades are the row-interleave) of solid rows — the horizontal dimension the model did not have.
A RHYTHM is a short list of relative band thicknesses that TILES across the burst — 1,1,3 reads thin, thin, thick, over and over, which is what the reference does and what a single spread curve cannot: spread is monotone, so it can only make the bands grow or shrink toward the horizon. With no weights the curve runs as before. Spread still shapes an unweighted burst. The burst is authored in PIXELS and the field takes the rest, so band thickness is the same on a phone and a desktop while the field absorbs the difference. The floor is where the ramp bottoms out — ~50% leaves the lower half for the parallax land layers, 100% is the above-the-clouds sky.
